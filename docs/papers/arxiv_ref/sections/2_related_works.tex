\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{MPPI and Deployable Local Controllers.} Nav2 MPPI \citep{nav2,mppi_orig}, TEB \citep{teb}, DWB, a Nav2 planner of the dynamic-window family \citep{dwa}, and Regulated Pure Pursuit \citep{rpp} are the standard deployable local controllers. MPPI's critic interface (\code{mppi::critics::CriticFunction}) exposes the per-rollout trajectories, the reference path, the goal and the furthest-reached path index, which are exactly the signals needed for entrapment detection, so a custom critic can be inserted without forking the optimizer. \sname{} uses this interface for escape and adds an output-side safety filter, instead of replacing the optimizer.

\noindent\textbf{Local-Minima Escape.} Most escape methods are always-on changes of the sampling distribution: log-MPPI \citep{log_mppi}, Tsallis-divergence MPC \citep{tsallis_mppi}, Stein-variational guidance \citep{svg_mppi}, ancillary-controller biasing \citep{biased_mppi}, and learned sampling distributions \citep{flow_mppi}. The closest detect-and-switch method is DRPA-MPPI \citep{drpa_mppi}, which detects entrapment from predicted trajectories and switches on a repulsive potential. It has no CBF or other formal safety, models no dynamic obstacles, and is not Nav2-integrated. \sname{} also detects and switches, but gates the escape through a safety certificate and adds a free-space gap subgoal that our ablation shows to be necessary.

\noindent\textbf{CBF--MPPI Safety.} Three fusion styles exist as research code: CBF terms in the rollout cost (Shield-MPPI \citep{shield_mppi}, which adds a local repair step, and BR-MPPI \citep{br_mppi}, which also steers sampling through a barrier-rate input), an output CBF-QP filter on the MPPI command (SCBF-MPPI \citep{scbf_mppi}, built on the CBFKit toolbox \citep{cbfkit}), and per-rollout safety filtering that makes every sample provably safe (GS-MPPI \citep{gs_mppi}, DualGuard \citep{dualguard}, which also filters the final command). For dynamic obstacles, DPCBF \citep{dpcbf} uses a parabolic safe set that is less conservative than collision-cone barriers but is QP-only and not embedded in MPPI. Our filter builds on the classical CBF-QP \citep{ames_cbf_tac,ames_cbf_ecc}. None of these works ships as a Nav2 controller (CBFKit builds a standalone node), and none addresses entrapment; \sname{} couples the barrier to the escape layer through its gain.

\noindent\textbf{Conformal Prediction for Safe Control.} The released controller also carries an online conformal margin that inflates the barrier radius under prediction uncertainty, drawing on conformal prediction for safe planning and control \citep{acp_yang,conformal_hri,lindemann,dixit} and on uncertainty-aware predictive barriers \citep{ua_pcbf}. This margin is follow-up work and is \emph{not} a contribution of this paper; every result reported here is isolated from it (\cref{sec:discussion}).
